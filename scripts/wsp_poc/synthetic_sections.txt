Z.1 ICT BUSINESS CONTINUITY AND RECOVERY

The Firm maintains a written ICT business continuity policy, approved by the
management body and reviewed at least annually, that forms an integral part of
the Firm's operational business continuity policy. The policy is implemented
through documented ICT response and recovery plans covering each critical or
important function.

The Firm's continuity arrangements are designed to ensure the continuity of
critical functions, to respond promptly and appropriately to all ICT-related
incidents so as to limit damage and prioritise the resumption of activities and
recovery actions, and to activate dedicated plans without delay that enable
containment measures, processes and technologies appropriate to each type of
ICT-related incident.

Recovery time objectives and recovery point objectives are set per function and
recorded in the continuity register. The Chief Information Security Officer
maintains an estimate of the aggregated annual costs and losses caused by
ICT-related disruptions, which is reported to the management body annually.

The Firm operates a dedicated crisis management function which, on activation,
sets out clear roles and responsibilities and coordinates internal and external
crisis communication, including with clients, counterparties, staff, and the
competent authority, in accordance with the Firm's communication plan.

Business continuity plans and ICT response and recovery plans are tested at
least yearly, and after every substantive change to ICT systems supporting
critical or important functions. Testing includes switchover to redundant
capacity and restoration from backup. Internal audit reviews the test results
and the Firm documents and remediates every deficiency identified. Crisis
communication procedures are included in the scope of testing.

The Firm maintains redundant ICT capacity with resources, capabilities and
functions adequate to its business needs, and maintains backup systems in a
physically and logically segregated environment from the primary systems.

Z.2 ICT-RELATED INCIDENT MANAGEMENT PROCESS

The Firm defines, establishes and implements an ICT-related incident management
process to detect, manage and notify ICT-related incidents. All ICT-related
incidents and all significant cyber threats are recorded in the incident
register, which is retained for five years.

The process sets out early warning indicators derived from monitoring
thresholds, and procedures to identify, track, log, categorise and classify
ICT-related incidents according to their priority, severity and the criticality
of the services affected.

Roles and responsibilities are assigned in advance for each incident type and
scenario, covering detection, triage, containment, eradication, recovery and
post-incident review. The Head of Technology Operations is the designated
incident manager; the Chief Compliance Officer is responsible for regulatory
notification.

Communication plans are established for staff, external stakeholders, the
media, affected clients, counterparties and, where required, the competent
authority. All ICT-related incidents classified as major are reported to senior
management and to the management body, together with an explanation of the
impact, the response and the remediation actions taken.

The Firm establishes and documents incident response procedures to mitigate
impacts and ensure that services become operational and secure in a timely
manner. Every major ICT-related incident is subject to a documented root cause
analysis, the findings of which are used to improve the Firm's ICT risk
management framework.

Z.3 SAFEKEEPING OF CLIENTS' CRYPTO-ASSETS AND FUNDS

Where the Firm holds crypto-assets belonging to clients, or the means of access
to such crypto-assets, the Firm makes adequate arrangements to safeguard the
ownership rights of clients, in particular in the event of the Firm's
insolvency, and to prevent the use of clients' crypto-assets on the Firm's own
account.

Client crypto-assets held on a distributed ledger are held at addresses
segregated from the addresses on which the Firm's own crypto-assets are held.
Client holdings are recorded in the Firm's books and records separately from
the Firm's own holdings, and positions are reconciled to the ledger daily.

Client funds are placed with a central bank or a credit institution by the end
of the business day following the day on which they were received. The Firm
takes all necessary steps to ensure that client funds so held are identifiable
separately from the Firm's own funds and from the funds of any other client.
The Firm does not use client funds on its own account under any circumstances.

Where the Firm provides payment services in relation to the crypto-asset
services it offers, it applies the safeguarding requirements applicable to
payment service providers to the funds concerned.

The means of access to client crypto-assets, including private keys and seed
phrases, are held under dual control with geographically distributed backups,
and the Firm's private key management procedure is reviewed annually by
internal audit.
